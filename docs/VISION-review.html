The core bet: scaffold, not modelevidence-settled

We build an orchestration layer around rented frontier models. No training, no fine-tuning.

Every competitive system found in the research — Halawi's pipeline, FutureSearch, Preseen, Bridgewater's AIA Forecaster — is a scaffold around frontier models: retrieval, prompting, aggregation, calibration. None trains its own model. This keeps the project solo-buildable and means each frontier model release upgrades us for free.

Trade-off accepted: our ceiling rides on vendor models and their pricing; we own the orchestration IP, not the intelligence.
Review prompts
  • Comfortable with full dependency on vendor models (cost, ToS, deprecation)?
  • Should cheap open-weight fine-tuning ever trigger revisiting this?

Evaluation-first methoddefault

The measurement harness ships before any architecture; no design is adopted without beating baseline on a leakage-hardened backtest.

Forecast feedback is brutally slow (months per resolution) and score gaps between designs are small (~0.01–0.03 Brier), so intuition can't adjudicate designs. The method: pre-registered experiments, paired Brier tests on frozen question sets, hundreds of questions per comparison, and a live tournament entry whose divergence from backtests flags a broken backtest.

Cost: slower first "real" agent. The discipline is the point, but it is a pace choice you should explicitly endorse.
Review prompts
  • Is the measured pace acceptable, or do you want a scrappier build-first loop?
  • Is "beats baseline with statistical significance" the right adoption bar?

Research & retrieval layerevidence-settled

Agentic news/web search, hard-filtered by date, feeding every forecasting pass.

Retrieval is the confirmed core of every competitive system; retrieval quality moved accuracy more than model choice in published work. Two design commitments: (1) retrieval is date-filtered so the agent only sees pre-forecast-time information (leakage guard), and (2) it targets high-quality news sources (AIA-style) rather than raw web.

Open cost question: paid data sources (news APIs, market data feeds) are not yet budgeted — free tiers first.
Review prompts
  • Any budget appetite for paid news/data APIs, or free-first until a rung is hit?

Ensemble mathevidence-settled

How many forecasts become one number: log-odds pooling, mild extremization, Platt calibration.

Three stacked, separately-confirmed layers:

  • Pool in log-odds space, never average probabilities (Satopää et al., validated on 1,300+ forecasters).
  • Extremize mildly or not at all — the classic √3 factor assumes independent information, but an LLM ensemble shares training data; shared information flips the optimum toward attenuation. Coefficient tuned on held-out backtest.
  • Platt-scale the output to counter systematic LLM hedging (AIA's confirmed technique).

Ensemble members are weighted by tracked performance — the mechanized version of "tracking", the best-evidenced human intervention. Outputs keep 1% granularity; rounding to 0.10 measurably hurt human superforecasters.

Review prompts
  • Any objection to the math layer being fixed formulas initially (vs. a reasoning agent — see candidate E)?

Market anchoring — be the additive ⅓evidence-settled

Where a market/community price exists, we model the adjustment, not the level.

The strongest 2025 system (AIA) loses to market consensus alone (Brier 0.126 vs 0.111) but a ~⅓-model/⅔-market ensemble beats the market (0.106). Halawi found the same shape. So on liquid questions our agent's job is to be additive information on top of the price — a humbling but confirmed framing.

Consequence: on liquid questions the final number will usually sit near the market's. The agent earns its keep on thin/absent markets and in the adjustment.
Review prompts
  • Comfortable that the system defers heavily to markets where they exist?
  • Does this change how interesting the project feels to you (edge vs. independence)?

Selective abstentionevidence-settled

The agent declines or defers on questions where it has no edge.

Halawi's system loses to the crowd overall but beats it when restricted to questions with crowd uncertainty 0.3–0.7 and good retrieval. Every pipeline pass therefore emits a confidence-in-forecast signal, and the system abstains (or just submits the community prior) below threshold. "Knowing when not to forecast" is a first-class feature, not a failure mode.

Review prompts
  • OK with an agent that sometimes answers "no edge, declining"?
  • In tournament play (where abstaining may cost score), submit the market/community prior instead?

Continuous re-forecastingevidence-settled

Forecasts are living values, updated on news, market moves, and schedule — never one-shot.

Updating frequency is the single strongest behavioral predictor of human forecasting accuracy (supers: 2.77→6.70 forecasts/question vs ~1.5). This mechanizes trivially but has real infrastructure implications: a question lifecycle store, news triggers, scheduled re-runs — and recurring inference cost per open question.

Review prompts
  • Any cost ceiling per question / per month we should design against?

Legible explanation as output contractdefault

The deliverable is a probability plus the argument for it — "here's why 23%".

Tournaments only score the number, so this is a deliberate product decision, not a scoring requirement. If your decomposition graph (hypothesis C) earns its keep, the explanation becomes a structured argument graph; otherwise structured prose synthesized from the winning passes. Arguably the more valuable half of the product for any real decision-making use.

Review prompts
  • Confirm: explanation is core product, worth cost even though tournaments ignore it?
  • Who is the explanation for — just you, or eventually shareable output?

Hypothesis C: your decomposition graphopen — steer this

The project's founding intuition, deliberately framed as a hypothesis rather than the design.

Your original idea — recursively decompose the question into knowable sub-facts, source data for each, weight by competence, bubble probabilities up a graph. The research pass found no direct evidence for or against it: the theoretical risk (conjunctive chains compound errors) stands but is undemonstrated, and our backtest would be among the first direct tests. Design guards if built: depth caps, a per-node outside-view anchor, and a holistic sanity-check against any multiplied chain.

Three possible fates, all currently live: (1) wins on accuracy outright, (2) loses on Brier but becomes the explanation layer over B/E's number, (3) serves as a diversity generator feeding ensemble B.

Review prompts

  • If it loses on accuracy, is the explanation-layer role a satisfying home for it?

Non-goals & domaindefault

What we deliberately are not building — each one reversible, but currently binding.
  • No trading. Score is Brier against resolutions, not P&L on Kalshi/Polymarket — despite the ACX article's $35→$2M hook being a trading story.
  • No fine-tuning / training. (See core bet.)
  • No long-horizon oracle questions ("AGI by 2035?") — we start where feedback and reference classes exist: binary, resolvable, months-scale questions.
  • Domain-agnostic within that — no vertical specialization (geopolitics, econ, sports) chosen yet, though specialization is a known lever.
Review prompts

  • Any domain you actually care most about forecasting? (Would shape question selection and retrieval sources.)
  • Long-horizon questions: permanently out, or a later phase?
